# Tells of machine-made interfaces

Check your page against these before finishing. Each is concrete enough to grep.

## Hard tells
1. Default violet/indigo as the accent, when the brief or design system supplies one.
2. A two-colour gradient hero.
3. Emoji standing in for icons (sparkles, rockets, targets, bolts, flames, bulbs)
   in headings, buttons, list items or icon slots. Use thin-stroke inline SVG that
   inherits `currentColor`.
4. Display headings in a hard-coded generic sans when the system supplies a serif
   or another display face.
5. A rounded card with a coloured stripe down its left edge. Drop the stripe or
   the radius.
6. Invented numbers: multipliers, uptime percentages, customer counts. Cite a real
   source or mark the figure as a placeholder.
7. Placeholder text left in: lorem ipsum, numbered stand-in headings, "sample content".
   A section with nothing to say needs better composition, not made-up words.

## Soft tells
- The unvaried Hero, Features, Pricing, FAQ, CTA sequence. Break it with at least
  one unexpected section.
- Remote placeholder-image services. Use supplied assets or a layout that needs none.
- A dozen raw hex values outside the token block: the palette was ignored.
- The accent showing up six or more times in the visible body.
- Decorative blobs or waves that carry no meaning.
- A perfectly symmetric layout with no tension. Alternate tight and airy sections.

## Adding character
Aim for about 80% familiar patterns and 20% distinctive choices, spent on:
one bold move (a typographic decision, a colour call, an unexpected proportion);
specific voice in microcopy ("Start tracking" over "Get started"); one
micro-interaction people remember; and one detail only a real user of this
product would have thought of.
A fair test: could a stranger tell which product the screenshot belongs to?
If not, it is still a template.
